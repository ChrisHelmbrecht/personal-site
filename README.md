# personal-site

Chris Helmbrecht's private website. A blue-ink skull is the navigation: every element in it is a door.

## Run it

No build step. Serve the folder (`python3 -m http.server`) and open it, or open `index.html` directly.

Reading time is counted from the text (220 words a minute).

More articles belong in the archive), `FRESH` (places kept for the newest, 3), `PER_PAGE` (archive, 12), `CATS` (the five main categories and their names in both languages).
  - **The overview** carries the whole list as plain HTML (picture, title, date, intro, link): that is what a browser without script, a search engine or an assistant sees. With the script it has two views of that same list. *Explore*: up to seven cards in fixed places, in three weights that mean something: the newest article is the large one ("Newest thought"), featured ones and then the next newest are the two middle ones, the rest are small. Three columns on a wide screen with room left free; below that the same cards in order of weight, two side by side on a phone. They sway a little (CSS only, transform; they rest in a hidden tab, out of sight, while a preview is open, during keyboard use, on "Pause motion", and do not exist with reduced motion). Resting the pointer on a card for a third of a second, a first tap, or keyboard focus opens a preview: the card itself rights itself and grows from where it hangs (its own picture and title; the short text, reading time and "Read article" follow once it is open), and folds back to the same place. The preview has one animation at a time: closing it while it still unfolds turns the same movement round, opening another card or resizing the window ends it at once, so it is either open or closed. The small card is not shown while its preview is open, so it reads as one object. Escape closes it and leaves the focus on the card without opening it again. While the keyboard's focus is on a card, its preview wears the focus ring (the focus itself stays on the card, so Enter still opens the article). A card reached with the keyboard while out of sight brings its whole preview into the window, not just the small card. The preview never gets taller than the window; a long text scrolls inside it while Close and "Read article" stay in reach. A plain click or Enter opens the article directly. Changing between Explore and Archive, the articles that are in both travel to their new places and the others fade in after them (Web Animations, transform and opacity only; with reduced motion the views simply change). *Archive*: everything, with search (titles, intros, tags; ä = ae = a, ß = ss), main categories, order and pages. The archive's state is in the address (`?view=archive&q=…&cat=…&sort=old&page=2`), so links can be shared and back/forward work; a view named in the address beats the one remembered for the visit.
  - **An article** has its own address, a link back that returns to the view, filters, page and scroll position one came from (kept in the tab's session; opened directly it is a plain link to the overview), the other language if it exists, a table of contents from four headings on, the reading time, and no movement except a thin line under the top edge that fills while one reads and is full where the text ends, not where the page ends (CSS scroll timeline; the script measures the end of the text; browsers without scroll timelines show nothing). The description for search engines and link previews is the teaser or the intro, cut at a sentence or a word, never inside one. Where the browser supports it, the clicked picture grows into the article's picture (view transitions; pure CSS plus one line of script, nothing waits for it).
  - `scripts/import-wp.py` brought the 32 articles (64 documents) over from the old WordPress site once; its comment says what it changed (the page builder's wrapping, empty paragraphs, players from other sites turned into links) and that the five main categories are a proposal folded from the old ones.

## The timeline

**Out of the navigation for now** (17 November 2026), while it is reworked. Its pages still answer at their addresses; nothing leads to them, and they are not in the sitemap or the llms files. To bring it back: `TIMELINE = True` in `scripts/siteconf.py` (menu without script, sitemap, llms files), `'timeline.html'` and its two names in the menu list at the top of `js/site.js`, the door on the skull in `index.html` (`<a class="spot" href="timeline.html" style="left:44%;top:62%;width:10%;height:9%;--d:1.5s"><b>Timeline</b></a>` in place of the `<span class="pathway" id="path">`), the sentence in the hidden text below it and its German in `content/de/index.txt`, the links in `bio.html` (with `content/de/bio.txt`) and `404.html`, and the line in `content/llms.txt`. Then run the build scripts.


- **One source.** Each year is one text document, `content/timeline/1997.txt`: a block of facts about the year, then one block per photograph, optionally with chapter blocks between them. The order of the blocks is the order on the page. The opening comment of `scripts/build-timeline.py` explains every field. `python3 scripts/build-timeline.py` (standard Python, nothing to install) makes `timeline.html` and `timeline/<year>.html` from them. **Never edit those pages by hand; they are overwritten.**
- **The originals stay outside the site.** They lie in one folder per year wherever they are kept (`<folder>/1997/anything.jpg`). `python3 scripts/timeline-pictures.py <folder>` (needs Pillow) takes in what is new: it gives each picture the next free id of its year (`1997-10`; an id never changes, links to single photographs use it), writes three web sizes into `assets/timeline/1997/` (`-s` 320 high, `-m` 640 high, `-l` 1800 on the long side; upright, sRGB, and without the camera's hidden data such as place and date) and adds a block at the end of the year's document.
- **To add photographs or a year:** put the originals into the year's folder, run `timeline-pictures.py <folder>`, then `build-timeline.py`, commit. A new year needs nothing but its folder.
  - *Order:* move the photo blocks in the year's document.
  - *Lead photograph and previews on the overview:* `lead:` and `previews:` in the year's first block (ids). Left empty, the first photographs in order are taken. The previews are cut square; `focus:` on a photograph says where its subject sits.
  - *Captions, places, dates, who took it:* the fields of the photo block. All optional; nothing is invented or translated by machine. A caption that exists in one language only is shown in that language.
  - *Chapters:* a block beginning with `chapter:` before the photographs that belong to it.
  - *To take a photograph out:* `draft: yes` in its block, build. It leaves the pages **and its web sizes are deleted from `assets/timeline/`**, so nothing of it lies in the site. To bring it back: `draft: no`, then `timeline-pictures.py` with the originals, build. `--hold "1986/Scan.jpg"` takes a new picture in as a draft from the start (`--hold-list FILE` for many, one `year/name | note` per line). A picture taken in as a draft is entered under a mark made from its file name (`file: #3f9c…`), not the name itself, because names can say who is in a picture. A `note:` line says why, for ourselves.
- **Settings** at the top of `build-timeline.py`: `SITE`, `LANGS` (English now; the German labels are written and wait for the site's language switch), `NEWEST_FIRST` (order of the years), `PREVIEWS` (beside the lead photograph, 4), `EAGER`, and `PLACEHOLDERS`: `None` now, so only years with photographs appear. Given a span of years (`(2008, 2026)`), the years in it that have no photographs yet are shown as empty frames ("Photos to come"); that was the stand-in while the later years were still being collected.
- **The overview** lists the years on one fine line, newest first: the year as a large numeral, optional title and places, the number of photographs, "Open year", a lead photograph shown whole and one, two or four previews. A click on a picture opens the album at that photograph (`timeline/1997.html#p-1997-03`); "Open year" opens it from its start. Every year can be linked (`timeline.html#1997`). The list of decades and years is one element: from 1000px a rail beside the years that marks the year in view and opens its decade, below that a "Choose year" button at the foot of the screen. It is a plain `<details>`, so it works without script. Only the overview's small pictures are fetched, the lower ones when they come near.
- **An album** shows every published photograph of the year as a contact sheet: rows of equal height, every photograph whole and in its order, frame numbers and captions beneath (pure CSS, no script places anything). Above it the way back, "Choose year" and the neighbouring years that have photographs.
- **The large view** is the album page's one `<dialog>`. The photograph grows from its place on the sheet and goes back there (Web Animations; with reduced motion it simply appears). Arrows on screen, arrow keys, Home/End and, as an extra, a one-finger swipe step through the year; nothing changes by itself. "Full size" shows the large file at its real size to move about in (a click on the picture does the same); a swipe does nothing then, nor while the page is pinched larger. After the last photograph the view stays open and offers "View again", the next year that has photographs, and "Back to the album". Closing returns to the sheet where it was, with the focus on the photograph that was opened. The open photograph is in the address (`?photo=1997-03`), so it can be linked; stepping replaces that address, and one step back closes the view. Only the photograph and its two neighbours are fetched, never the year. A large file that fails says so and offers to try again.
- **Coming back.** The album's own link goes to `timeline.html#1997`; if one came from the overview, it returns to the exact place (kept in the tab's session). Opened directly, it is a plain link. Where the browser supports view transitions, the clicked picture or the year's numeral carries over between overview and album; nothing depends on it.
- **Without script** the overview, the year list and the albums work, and each photograph is a link to its large file.
- **Trying things out** with made-up years (one, five, thirty and 120 photographs, long captions, chapters, many years), never in the site: `python3 scripts/build-timeline.py --out <a copy of the site> --synthetic`, or `--only 3` for just a few years.
- The documents in `content/` are not served. The repository is public, though: whatever is committed here can be seen by anyone, the web sizes included. A photograph that should not be public must be a draft **before** it is committed.

## Two languages

## Sound

The sound switch is kept in memory first (`window.siteSound` in `js/site.js`: `off()`, `asked()`, `set()`), and only also written to the browser's storage. Every script asks `siteSound`, never the storage itself: a browser that refuses to store anything (Brave with all cookies blocked, some private windows) must still obey the switch. There the choice, like the chosen language, travels with the tab in `window.name` (`sound:off;lang:en`).

- One switch for the site: the button in the header of the start page and the bio, kept as `sound` in the browser's storage (`off` silences every page). The two arcade games and the flying lesson have a switch of their own.
- Browsers let sound out only inside a tap, click or key press. `window.siteSound.wake(ctx)` (in `js/site.js`) does what is needed at that moment and is called wherever a sound begins.
- An iPhone whose ringer switch is off keeps a web page's sounds silent, though not a film's. `siteSound.loud()` asks the phone to treat the page's sound like a film's. It is called only for someone who has switched sound **on by hand**; a visitor who never asked keeps a silent phone silent and their own music playing. On the bio, switching sound on answers with a short soft note, so one hears at once that sound comes out.
- A phone puts the sound to sleep when the page is left; back on the page, the next touch wakes it.
- The start page's switch answers at once and stops the sound by more than one means. It shows "on" from the moment the recording is on its way, so a tap while it still loads refuses it. Switching off fades out, then unplugs the sound and stops its clock (`apply()` in `js/site.js`); every step is guarded on its own (`safe()`), so one that fails cannot keep the button from answering.
- The sound button takes a tap from the lifted finger as well as from the click that follows, counted once (`onPress()`).
- **Sound check.** The start page opened with `#soundcheck` at the end of its address shows a small readout: which events the button receives, whether the sound has started, is loading or muted, the state of its source, where the button sits, and the last error. It is there for phones that cannot be inspected from outside (first used for Brave on an iPhone, November 2026). Nothing of it runs without that mark; it can be removed once it is no longer needed.

## How the bio behaves

- **Hidden things are real controls.** The helicopter and the snowboarder are buttons, Tenerife has an invisible button over its engraving, New York an invisible link. Nothing points at them, but a finger, a mouse and the keyboard all reach them, and each has a name for screen readers. Every engraving exists twice (beside the text on wide screens, in the text on narrow ones); only the copy that is displayed can be reached. Beside the text only one engraving is on show at a time, so focusing a control in another one brings its chapter up (`bring` in `js/bio.js`).
- **The flying lesson has two ways in.** Catching the helicopter in flight stays. The first click also leaves an invitation beside it for a while (“Sie wollten doch Pilot werden.” · Flying lesson), an ordinary button. With reduced motion, in data-saving mode or when the film cannot be shown, the helicopter does not fly and the invitation is the way in.
- **One sound switch.** `localStorage.sound` is `off` when the visitor has muted the site, on the start page or with the same button in the bio's header. The bio's fly-by obeys it, the 360° film starts muted then, and the flying lesson starts silent until its own switch (`flight-sound`) has been used.
- **One rule for films.** A film runs only while its engraving is on show, the tab is visible, the page has been touched in the last three minutes and no pop-up lies over it (`sync` in `js/bio.js`).
- **Loading and failing are said.** When the game or the film viewer takes a moment to load, a small note says so; when loading fails, it says so and offers another try. A graphics chip that refuses the shader gets the still picture.
